# AdSense Review Report — toolnest.quest

**Date:** 2026-09-28
**Source:** Google AdSense Help Community thread, "ToolNest.quest repeatedly rejected for 'Low value content'"
**Status:** Rejected twice, about 15 days apart. Reason given: **Low value content**.

---

## What I asked

- Why the site keeps getting "Low value content" even after improvements.
- The site had 64 articles on AI tools, dev tools and productivity, plus About, Contact, Privacy, Terms, Disclaimer, a sitemap, category pages and author attribution.
- I asked which of these is the likely cause: originality, depth, AI content, lack of first-hand experience, structure, repetitive topics, author info, traffic/indexing, ad placement, or something else.

---

>

### busterjet, follow-up

They quoted this line from our author/About text:

> *"Palak is an IT engineer, developer and researcher, and runs ToolNest. He uses the software he writes about in his own development work and says so when he has not. Each article is checked against vendor documentation, changelogs and pricing pages; drafts may start with AI assistance, but he checks and edits every claim before it goes live."*

> This confirms the suspicions of AI generated, or otherwise unoriginal content.
>
> Google Search probably won't index or rank this type of content.

### My last question

> If I now make significant changes to the site's content, rewrite the articles with genuine human input and improve the overall content quality, can I submit the site for AdSense review again? Or, after being rejected twice, is it better not to apply again?

### Reply

> You can apply any time you like, unless they limit the timeframe.
>
> But over 90% of sites are rejected, and almost all that have an overdone theme such as IT/tech.
>
> If you think your visitors would appreciate rewritten articles, then go ahead. But don't do anything if the only motivation is the hope of Adsense.

**Takeaway:** There's no ban on reapplying. The odds are low for any IT/tech site, though. Write for readers, and treat AdSense as a side effect.

## What the reviews actually say

| Issue | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Site structure, policy pages, navigation | **Not the problem.** Nobody raised it. |
| AI-generated look | **Main problem.** They see it across the whole site, not in a few articles. |
| First-hand experience | **Missing.** There are no screenshots, measurements or real usage results. |
| Authority | **Missing.** Nothing shows why this author should be trusted. |
| Organic traffic | **None.** With no search traffic, there's nothing for AdSense to monetize. |
| Author/About wording | **Hurts us.** The "drafts may start with AI assistance" line reads like an admission. |
| Forum posts | Also read as AI-written, which weakens trust further. |

## Action plan

1. **Rewrite the About/author text.** Remove the AI-drafting explanation. Describe what I actually use: my stack, my projects, the tools I pay for.
2. **Don't mass-rewrite the 61 old articles.** Rewriting them in one go is another batch, and batches are the fingerprint. Noindex or remove the weakest ones. Keep only the ones where I can add my own screenshot or a number I measured.
3. **Write new articles by the CLAUDE.md article rule.** Each one needs a real data point, my own screenshot, and an honest downside. Maximum 2 per week.
4. **Wait for search signal before reapplying.** Wait until Google Search Console shows real impressions and clicks. Expect about 2–3 months, not days.
5. **Write public text myself, short and plain.** That covers forum replies, the About page, and the experience paragraph in each article.

## Can I reapply?

Yes. Two rejections don't block the site. But reapplying to the same site in its current state will get the same answer. Reapply only after steps 1–4 are visibly done.
